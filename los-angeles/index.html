Preventing Water Damage in Los Angeles Homes

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Los Angeles homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. A musty smell in a closet, a toilet that rocks, or a tap that drips belongs on the list too.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Los Angeles home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Water Damage We See Most in Los Angeles

In Los Angeles, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. The crew from Huntington Beach Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Los Angeles began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

The First Hour After Water Damage in Los Angeles

The right first steps change with the type of water on your floor. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. It is fine to step through in footwear, towel it up and carry your things clear. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

In every case, there are simple ways to save the crew time when it reaches Los Angeles. Clear a path from the door to the wet area and move cars so the truck can park close. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. After it has sat in building materials it degrades, and mold can take hold in 24 to 48 hours. So even for a leak in Los Angeles that looks minor, the first hour is the one to use well.
